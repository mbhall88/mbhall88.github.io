The problem

Same bottleneck, different fields

Immunology, cancer biology, infectious disease. Different questions, same two problems.

Too much to read

More papers than any of us has time to read, in every subfield.

Reference managers store. They don't think.

Zotero, Mendeley and EndNote organise your library. They don't help you interrogate it.

Meeting notes go missing

Supervision and collaboration meetings fade into memory, or into scattered voice memos.

One tool, two gaps

Gemini Notebook has become how I close both. It's not a replacement for reading. It's a way to question a curated set of sources.

The basics

What it is

A Google tool, built on Gemini, that answers only from the sources you upload into a notebook. Ask questions in plain language and it retrieves the relevant passages before answering (retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG).

The obvious objection

Does it hallucinate?

Why the risk drops

Every claim comes with an inline citation to the exact passage and source it used.

It's restricted to your uploaded sources rather than the open web or training data, so hallucination risk drops sharply versus a general chatbot.

Still on you

It can't tell a good source from a bad one. A flawed or biased paper gets reflected back faithfully.

Grounded ≠ correct. It's only as good as what you feed it, so click through the citations for anything you'll rely on.

Where to be careful

  • Not a substitute for reading. It retrieves and summarises, but it doesn't judge quality or novelty for you.
  • Source limits vary by plan (see Plans), plus a 500,000-word / 200 MB cap per source.
  • No live web access from inside a notebook. It won't know about anything published after you added your sources.
  • Sharing a notebook shares your source limit. Collaborators don't get their own quota.

Data & privacy. Uploaded sources go to Google's servers under your Google account. Before uploading, check what's appropriate for unpublished data, patient information, or confidential meeting content, and check your institution's data-handling guidance.
